Overview

The C-130 Hercules is a four-engine turboprop military transport aircraft. It first flew in 1954 and is still in production today, which makes it one of the longest-serving aircraft in history.

It can carry troops, vehicles and heavy cargo, and it can take off and land on short, rough runways. Because of this, it is used in more than 60 countries for airlifts, airdrops, medical evacuation, search and rescue, and firefighting.

Main Components of the C-130 Hercules

  • Fuselage: The main body. It holds the flight deck at the front and a large cargo compartment behind it.
  • Wings: The wings are mounted high on the fuselage and have four turboprop engines that provide lift and thrust.
  • Landing Gear: The landing gear consists of features rugged, high-flotation landing gear designed for versatile operations, allowing it to taxi, take off, and land on rough, unprepared airstrips while supporting heavy cargo loads.
  • Tail: The tail has a vertical stabilizer and a horizontal stabilizer that help keep the plane stable in flight.
  • Propellers: The propellers are attached to the engines and provide thrust to move the plane forward.
  • Avionics: The avionics include the navigation, communication, and flight control systems that help the pilots fly the plane safely.

History

In the early 1950s, the US Air Force asked for a rugged transport that could land on short, rough runways. Lockheed won the contract, and the prototype first flew on 23 August 1954. The first production aircraft, the C-130A, entered service in 1956.

Since then, the C-130 has been built in many versions and used by air forces around the world. Over 2,500 have been built, and it has been in continuous production for more than 70 years.

Iconic versions

C-130A (1956):
The first production version of the C-130 Hercules.
C-130E (1960s):
Longer range, thanks to external fuel, tanks under the wings. It was widely used in the Vietnam War. An improved version with better avionics and a more powerful engine.
C-130H (1970s):
The most common version worldwide, with a more powerful engine and updated systems.
AC-130 Spectre/Spooky:
A gunship with side-firing guns and cannons, first used in Vietnam.
KC-130:
An aerial refueling tanker, used by the US Marine Corps.
LC-130:
Fitted with skis for landing on snow and ice, used in Antarctica and Greenland.
WC-130:
The “Hurricane Hunter”, which flies into storms to collect weather data.
MC-130:
Special operations version, used for infiltration, exfiltration, and resupply of special forces.
C-130J Super Hercules (1990s):
The most recent version, featuring advanced avionics and a more powerful engine.

C-130 in the Portuguese Air Force (FAP)

  1. September 1977: The first C-130H arrives and Squadron 501 "Bisontes" is formed at Air Base No. 6 (Montijo).
  2. 1977-1978: The Air Force receives five C-130H aircraft in total.
  3. 1983: Squadron 501 starts taking part in the "Volant Rodeo" airlift competition.
  4. 1991-1992: One C-130H-30 is added, and two older aircraft are stretched.
  5. 2004: Portugal shows interest in the C-130J Super Hercules.
  6. Today: The KC-390 is replacing the Hercules at Montijo.

Fun Facts

Can a C-130 land on an aircraft carrier?

Yes. In 1963, a US Navy KC-130F made a series of landings and takeoffs on the carrier USS Forrestal. It used no arresting gear and no catapult. This was a test, and the C-130 never became a carrier aircraft.

What is "Fat Albert"?

Fat Albert is the C-130 that supports the US Navy Blue Angels flight demonstration team. It carries crew, equipment and spare parts from show to show.

Which aircraft flies into hurricanes?

The WC-130, known as the "Hurricane Hunter". It flies straight into storms to measure wind, pressure and temperature, which helps forecasters predict where a hurricane will go.

Can a C-130 fight forest fires?

Yes. With a MAFFS kit (Modular Airborne Fire Fighting System) loaded in the cargo bay, a C-130 can drop fire retardant over a fire. The Portuguese Air Force uses this system in the summer.

Why is it called "Bisontes" in Portugal?

"Bisontes" (Bison) is the nickname of Squadron 501, the Portuguese Air Force unit that operates the C-130 at Montijo.

How long has it been in production?

Since the 1950s. It is one of the longest continuously produced military aircraft in history.
